# Experiment 24 — competition-only MRI preflight

Status: controlled ablation specified; no long GPU training launched or falsely labeled as an exact reproduction.

Goal: Keep the 23A/23B CoAtNet/MIL + Compact S6 ensemble structure, replacing only its OAI-trained Raptor classification checkpoint with one trained on RSNA competition MRI and report-derived labels. Public ImageNet initialization is allowed; OAI and other external MRI are excluded. Freeze S6 and its inference code for this first ablation. This is a **data-source and checkpoint change**, not a claim that the 0.954 model has been reproduced exactly.

Baseline: 23A and 23B each score 0.954 Public. 23B receipt: 3 visible studies, zero fallbacks, 209.9 seconds. These are visible-only diagnostics, not hidden cohort metrics.

Critical provenance: 23B's Compact manifest says four ConvNeXt-small checkpoints were trained on 4,344 competition studies, with 58 gold studies excluded. It still loads anatomy and side auxiliary weights whose training provenance must be checked before calling the complete pipeline competition-only. The Raptor checkpoint in 23A/B includes OAI training. Thus the first test is "competition-only *Raptor classification training* + frozen S6", not yet a certified end-to-end competition-only submission.

Training gate before GPU: verify a 96-slice, 384-pixel competition-only cache (or reproducible build path), the report-derived target table, exact study IDs, group-disjoint folds, public ImageNet initialization hash, and model/auxiliary training provenance. A 224-pixel 05A cache or 44-slice public Raptor corpus is not equivalent to the scored 96-slice recipe. Public training notebooks currently document a 44-slice/336-pixel competition-only CoAtNet baseline; that is useful as source code but not an exact replacement. Stop if any source is missing or ambiguous.

Compute gate: run a bounded subset of studies and one epoch/one fold; record RSS, VRAM, throughput, failures and projected full run time. No full training until the estimate fits the available GPU quota and outputs can resume from per-epoch checkpoints.

24A result (2026-10-13): the Kaggle competition mount has 4,407 unique training studies, of which 58 have all twelve gold labels; 4,349 are report-only. No extra labels or MRI cache were attached. A raw 96×384×384 uint8 volume for each of the 4,349 weak studies would be 57.335 GiB, while `/kaggle/working` had only 19.502 GiB free in that CPU run. Therefore a monolithic uncompressed cache in working storage is ruled out for that environment. This does **not** establish that compressed/sharded storage or streaming training is viable; measure those on a small deterministic sample first. 24A made no model-quality measurement and did not change the 0.954 benchmark.

24B result (2026-10-13): six weak training studies spanning 3–14 series all decoded without failure (62–96 valid slices), 24.95 seconds total including setup. The representative 96×384 `np.savez_compressed` volume measured 7.10–11.25 MB per study; the six-study median projects to 42.2 GiB for 4,349 weak studies, larger than 19.502 GiB working space. The notebook's 4.43-hour single-process projection is for a **single preprocessing/compression pass**, not a full training ETA. Six deliberately series-count-spread studies do not prove full-set distribution or exact byte identity with the 0.954 author's corpus. Do not create a monolithic cache or repeat DICOM decoding every epoch as a default plan.

Local label audit: the saved notebook 02 `L1_family_mean_RECOMMENDED.csv` contains 4,349 unique weak-study IDs. Its source manifest marks one of eight sources `REFERENCE_LEAKY` and the notebook filters that family before constructing L1. Remaining sources are public report-label tables; before calling the whole experiment strictly competition-only, verify that each was generated solely from competition reports and does not embed other MRI or gold labels. Do not train on any of the 58 gold rows.

Public reference check: the published Dread Development training corpus is 44×336 and explicitly *not* the later 96×384 geometry. The similarly named “Native 384 Dense Corpus” Kaggle dataset appears to contain a ~272 MB checkpoint rather than 4,349 cached study volumes. The 0.954 author's public discussion reports a competition-only CoAtNet recipe around 0.942 public and an OAI-augmented recipe around 0.949 standalone, but the runs are not compute-matched; removing OAI is not evidence of an expected score increase. An exact 96×384 competition-only retrain would require sharded competition-derived cache in read-only Kaggle inputs and resumable training, followed by honest paired evaluation before blending into 23A/B.

Storage execution candidate: Kaggle's published notebook output quota is 20 GB and the actual 24A/24B working free space was 19.502 GiB. A four-shard partition of 4,349 weak studies would average roughly 10.6 GiB compressed per shard under 24B's **very small-sample** estimate; each shard should have a hard output guard and separate saved version, with chunked archives so incomplete runs can be diagnosed. Do not launch four long shard jobs until one shard builder has passed a short resume/identity test and the downstream CoAtNet training notebook can actually consume the format. Public `timm/coatnet_rmlp_2_rw_384.sw_in12k_ft_in1k` ImageNet-12k/1k initialization is available from Hugging Face; it must be pinned by content hash and loaded without external MRI weights. Training feasibility also requires a small bounded GPU backward-pass benchmark before accumulating all four shards.

24C result (2026-10-13): shard 0 completed on Kaggle CPU in 3,642.74 seconds, with 1,088/1,088 assigned weak studies, 11 ZIP chunks and 10.223 GiB of NPZ payload, below the 16 GiB guard. The pasted manifest has 1,088 distinct sorted UIDs and entries, no failed DICOM slices, and a recomputed assigned-UID SHA-256 matching the receipt. Chunk counts are ten groups of 100 plus 88. Valid nonzero-slice counts are 74 (32 studies), 83 (1), 84 (269), 95 (1), and 96 (785). Two studies have one more decoded slice than nonzero image/mask slice; this is compatible with an all-zero normalized image, not by itself a DICOM read failure. The manifest and receipt alone do **not** prove that saved ZIP payloads can be read back or that SHA-256 digests match the actual NPZ bytes.

Next gate: `24D_Competition_Only_Cache_Shard0_Readback_CPU.ipynb` attaches only the saved 24C shard-0 Output and streams every archived NPZ to verify CRC, byte count and manifest SHA-256, then decodes a bounded sample for shape/dtype/mask. It writes a small JSON report. Do not produce shards 1–3 unless this report says `PASS`. This audit still does not establish training quality or exact equivalence to the public author's preprocessing; inference must reproduce the newly built cache recipe.

Evaluation gate: compare per-target OOF macro AUC and paired bootstrap intervals on group-disjoint folds. Use the 58 official gold as an audit only, not for checkpoint or blend tuning. Keep 23A unchanged; train a new competition-only CoAtNet branch, then pre-register the original 70/30 blend with Compact S6 for a Public probe if validation supports it. Do not infer 0.96+ from removing OAI alone. A true *data-only* comparison also requires an audit/replacement of S6's auxiliary anatomy/side weights.
